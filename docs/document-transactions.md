# Cross-page ink and document history

The scrolling finite-page editor captures drawing, erasing and lasso contacts across hosted paper and sticky-note surfaces. The originating native view retains pointer ownership, palm rejection, pressure/history smoothing and cancellation. `DocumentInkCapture` freezes geometry at contact-down, partitions segments at every paper/note edge and interpolates pressure. Ink on a note is stored in note-local coordinates. Width adjusts between page scales to preserve screen thickness. Gaps contain no ink; re-entering a surface creates another segment of the same logical action. Shapes and graphs are generated in source coordinates and then split as line outlines, preserving their styling. Ruler projections, hold-to-shape and scribble erasing use the same routing. Preview updates go directly to native views. Layout changes or unavailable crossed pages discard the complete contact; locked surfaces reject drawing and protect erasing.

Pen drawing, finger erasing and lasso use document capture after the normal input/palm gate admits them. Finger drawing keeps its original page ownership so retrospective palm withdrawal remains intact. Single sheets and infinite canvases use the same pure routing for sticky-note boundaries and commit their combined page/note edit through the page content mutation. Ordinary single-page drawing without a note crossing keeps the original commit path. Cross-page capture does not load the entire notebook or run Compose state updates per sample. Eraser previews consume each new path segment once, rather than replaying the full gesture. Laser contacts forward session-only samples to neighboring native views and never produce a content edit.

A spanning lasso selects editable ink, text, sticky notes and images across frozen hosted workspaces, including notes outside the paper. Hidden and locked layers remain protected. One shared bar supports Copy, Duplicate, Delete, ink restyling, handwriting-to-text, drag, resize, rotate and Move to page. Restyling and recognition include the ink inside selected sticky notes. Recognition completes before a revision-checked grouped commit. Dragging translates the whole selection; resizing/rotation uses a common document pivot and routes transformed outlines back to destination surfaces; selected text, notes and pictures retain their complete object and move to the page beneath their centre. Selected pages must be hosted for these geometric actions. Layer changes act on the entire selection when the destination layer is editable on every member. The shared page picker performs a grouped move, including copied image assets and destination fitting. Per-page transform handles remain suppressed for a spanning selection.

## PDF marking regions

A mark-area drag spanning hosted PDF pages creates one linked manual region, with a page-local rectangle on each crossed sheet. `DocumentMarking` intersects the document rectangle with each paper frame; it excludes seams and keeps mixed PDF scales in page units. The page where the drag began owns the single award, including upward drags. The allocation popover shows the number of pages and reads candidate allocations from every segment; ambiguous guesses are left for the marker to choose. Tapping or hovering any segment opens the same award, loading its anchor page when necessary.

`MarkZones.parts` keys segments by notebook page ID and PDF index, so reordering a page or duplicating the same PDF sheet cannot redirect the award to an unrelated page. Manual-zone preferences retain their existing six-field encoding for single-page zones; linked regions append a seventh field with escaped page IDs and local segment bounds. Existing single-page rows remain readable and unchanged. This extends only marking preferences, not the notebook, snapshot or journal formats. Older builds do not display the extended region definitions; their ordinary award text and decorations remain ordinary page content.

Awarding, changing or clearing a linked region loads and revision-checks every member, then commits one document group. Only the anchor gets an awarded-mark text box, so totals count the award once. Continuations receive the matching box/strike decoration and retain their own unrelated mark stamps. Grouped Undo/Redo restores all member content together. Editing the allocation keeps the linked geometry; removing its definition clears the shared award. Definitions retain the existing preference behavior and are separate from the content undo ledger.

`node tools/marking-smoke.cjs` checks mixed scales, upward capture, unavailable pages, page identity, old/new preference round trips, one award, re-awards, clearing and exact inverse edits. On device, drag across adjacent PDF pages in both directions, confirm one allocation, tap a continuation, award/change/clear it, then Undo/Redo and reopen the notebook. Check that the total counts one award, no unrelated continuation stamp is changed, and an unloaded or locked member cannot produce a partial award.

## Durable commit and compatibility

`FolioViewModel.commitDocumentEdits` validates member revisions, computes positional forward/inverse edits and publishes all member content and history together. The notebook ledger records group membership and action sequence. Undo/Redo loads unloaded members with their exact content/history checkpoint, validates every member stack, and queues one document operation. A newer ordinary member edit must be undone before its group. Structure actions retain their existing session-only history; grouped content history survives restart.

`DocumentReducer.history` plans the complete replay before live stacks change. It requires the selected ledger step and every member top's group/action identity to agree, then builds only affected pages, inverse stack effects and the moved ledger in current page order. The engine applies the successful plan to live stacks and indexed state. Normal and grouped loading install page history before publishing loaded content. Cached group availability depends only on the ledger and page membership; content edits reuse it without retaining old ink.

The repository reserves member journal sequences, atomically writes and fsyncs a CRC-protected `document.pending` intent, appends/fsyncs each missing member record, checkpoints the notebook ledger and affected page summaries, then removes the intent. Notebook directory changes are fsynced. Recovery repeats these steps safely after interruption; already-applied records are not duplicated. A conflicting reserved sequence fails recovery. Startup and backup staging recover pending commits before exposing their state. Failed save operations remain queued in order for Retry; backup barriers report the failure instead of exporting uncommitted state.

An index remains version 6 until its first grouped action, then permanently writes version 7. Group/action metadata uses edit flag 16, journal record version 2 and snapshot version 3. Other journals and snapshots keep their existing versions. Snapshot version 3 includes the layer-list slot even when empty. An older build cannot safely read a notebook that uses grouped history and rejects its version 7 index. Portable native v3 backups preserve the ledger, snapshots and journals; pending intents are recovered before staging. Duplicating a notebook resets its history as before.

## Verification

`node tools/backup-smoke.cjs` exercises page/note clipping, interpolated pressure, mixed scales, shape and styled graph outlines, swept/incremental erasing, protected underlying ink, local canvas coordinates, skipped boundaries, re-entry, blocked pages, spanning lasso, binary/JSON identity, interruption before/after every member append/checkpoint/completion, repeated recovery, history folding, grouped Undo/Redo, legacy version selection and a portable ZIP round-trip with every member history preserved. Navigation, writing-follow, palm-rejection, music and graph smoke runners check adjacent pure rules. These checks do not validate Android filesystem power-loss behavior or native device input.

Build a signed local APK with `./build.sh -n -- :app:bundleReleaseClassesToCompileJar :app:prepareBackupSmoke`. Publication must wait for the storage/device round-trip required by AGENTS.md. No device was connected during this implementation.

## Required device round-trip

1. Cross two and three finite pages with pen/highlighter, varied pressure, coalesced history, unequal PDF scales and visible seams both on/off. Check gaps, dots and page re-entry. Test a resting palm, canceled contact, notebook/layout change, pane resize and Undo during contact; no partial or duplicate ink should remain.
2. Draw pen/highlighter, ruler lines, every shape and styled graphs across paper and sticky edges, starting both inside and outside notes. Erase across the same boundaries with pressure and whole-stroke modes; locked note paper must protect the ink beneath it. Hold-to-shape and scribble erasing must retain surface ownership. Check that cancellation leaves every page unchanged. Lasso ink, text, sticky notes and images on adjacent pages. Check hidden/locked layers, shared copy/duplicate/delete/restyle/layer actions, dragging, resize/rotate and handwriting-to-text and a move into a loaded destination. Undo and Redo must affect every member together, including images.
3. Close/reopen and restart after a grouped stroke, Undo and Redo. Scroll member pages out of the loaded window before replay. Place an ordinary edit on one member and verify it is undone before the group. Delete/restore a member with structure Undo and recheck group membership.
4. Interrupt the app during group writes and after Undo/Redo, then reopen. Compare member content, stack identities, index revisions and pending-intent cleanup. Simulate a failed write and Retry; later queued edits must remain ordered and backups must report failure until saved.
5. Export/import a native notebook and restore a library backup after both grouped Undo and Redo. Compare all member contents and perform Undo/Redo again. Open legacy base/layer notebooks and verify unchanged behavior and conversion. Verify an older build refuses a grouped notebook rather than partially opening it.
6. Record native pen latency, sustained-writing saves, long-document memory and dense-page scrolling on the same device/notebooks before and after this build.
